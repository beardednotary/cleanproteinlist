Amazon Sued Over Orgain Protein Powder: Lead, Cadmium & Arsenic

Published July 31, 2026 | Breaking Legal News | Class Action Lawsuit

Direct Answer: What is the Amazon Orgain lawsuit?

On July 31, 2026, consumers filed a proposed class action against Amazon.com Inc. alleging the retailer sold Orgain Organic Vegan Protein products without disclosing alleged lead, cadmium and arsenic contamination. The complaint's own testing table reports levels as high as 37.1 ppb lead, 87.9 ppb cadmium, and 18.8 ppb arsenic across the four named products. There is no recall, the allegations are unproven, and if you still buy supplements on Amazon the practical move is to switch to a better-tested alternative.

CLASS ACTION LAWSUIT FILED · JULY 31, 2026

Amazon is now the latest retailer pulled into the Orgain heavy-metals story. The complaint says Amazon used clean-label marketing language to sell Orgain while failing to disclose that testing allegedly detected toxic metals in the product.

Editorial note: This article summarizes publicly reported allegations in a pending lawsuit. The claims are unproven and have not been decided by any court. Nothing here is legal, medical, or financial advice.

Amazon Orgain Class Action: Case Details

Defendant: Amazon.com Inc.
Court: U.S. District Court, Western District of Washington (Seattle)
Case caption: Cada Et Al v. Amazon.com Inc.
Case number: 2:26-cv-02704
Filed: July 31, 2026
Plaintiffs' firm: Hagens Berman
Product: Orgain Organic Vegan Protein + 50 Superfoods and Vegan Protein Powder (Vanilla Bean, Creamy Chocolate Fudge)
Metals alleged: Lead (up to 37.1 ppb), cadmium (up to 87.9 ppb), arsenic (up to 18.8 ppb)
Notable allegation: The press release highlights one buyer who allegedly purchased 36 containers for about $1,080 total
Claims: Consumer protection and deceptive omission claims
Relief sought: Damages, restitution, injunctive relief, and jury trial

What the Lawsuit Alleges

This is not a poisoning case in the classic sense. The complaint instead frames Amazon's conduct as an omission and deception problem: shoppers were allegedly shown clean, wholesome marketing language while never being told that lab testing could detect heavy metals in the product they were buying.

What Amazon Allegedly Promoted vs. What Testing Reported

What the complaint says Amazon shoppers were shown:

  • "Cleaner Ingredients"
  • "Quality Ingredients, Higher Standards"
  • "Feel Good Energy"
  • USDA Organic and clean-label positioning that implies purity

What testing cited in the complaint reports:

  • Lead up to 37.1 ppb
  • Cadmium up to 87.9 ppb
  • Arsenic up to 18.8 ppb

The theory is simple: buyers cannot detect heavy metals themselves, so the duty to disclose allegedly sat with the seller.

This is where headlines often get sloppy. The complaint leans on independent testing plus the broader context from Consumer Reports and the Clean Label Project. Those are not the same thing, and readers should keep the yardsticks separate.

Why Orgain Keeps Landing in These Stories

This is not really an "Amazon problem" in isolation. It is the same pattern we have been flagging for months: organic plant proteins keep showing up in heavy-metal disputes because plants absorb minerals and metals from soil, and the concentration process amplifies whatever was there.

That is why the USDA Organic label does not solve the issue. Organic standards regulate farming practices and prohibited substances, not heavy-metal content. If shoppers read "organic" as "screened for lead," they are reading far more into the seal than it actually guarantees.

This is now an Orgain retailer wave

The Amazon filing follows the Costco / Orgain lawsuit filed on July 7, 2026. Same brand, same core contamination story, different retailer. That is exactly why this one matters: it suggests the legal theory is expanding from manufacturer-adjacent claims to marketplace sellers too.

What Amazon and Orgain Have Said

Hagens Berman's July 31, 2026 materials focus on the plaintiffs' allegations. We did not see an Amazon response included there. Orgain has previously said, in response to the earlier Costco-linked case, that its products are safe to consume and comply with applicable food-safety standards even though trace naturally occurring substances can appear in plant-based ingredients.

Should You Stop Buying Orgain on Amazon?

The calm answer is the same one we gave on Costco: do not panic, but do not ignore the pattern either.

  • If you use Orgain daily: the conservative move is to switch now, especially if you are pregnant, nursing, buying for kids, or stacking multiple plant-based supplements.
  • If you use it occasionally: this is more of a "do not make it your default" signal than an emergency recall situation.
  • If you still shop on Amazon: do not just replace one unverified plant protein with another. Swap to a product with stronger testing or certification support.

Why This Matters Beyond One Amazon Listing

This case matters because Amazon is not a niche seller. If plaintiffs can keep pushing this retailer-disclosure theory, it broadens the heavy-metals conversation from "which brand is dirty?" to "what does the platform have to tell shoppers?" That is a meaningful shift.

It also keeps pressure on the larger policy story. California's proposed SB 1033 would force batch-level testing and disclosure. Cases like this help explain why that push is gaining attention.
