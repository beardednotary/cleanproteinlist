Is Ascent Protein Powder Safe? Native Whey Lead Testing (2026)

Published January 19, 2026 | Updated October 21, 2026 | 7 min read | By Ray Rothwell

❓ Direct Answer: Is Ascent Protein Safe?

Unknown. No lead figure for Ascent has been published. Consumer Reports hasn’t tested it. Clean Label Project included Ascent Native Fuel Whey (Chocolate) in its 165-product protein study, but it wasn’t among the 16 products CLP found lowest in contaminants, and CLP doesn’t publish the individual numbers. Ascent’s whey is Informed Sport certified, which covers banned substances, not lead. Working in its favor: whey tested lowest of any protein type in CR’s data.

What the Clean Label Project result means

CLP tested 165 protein powders for heavy metals and other contaminants, then named the 16 with the lowest levels as its Clean Sixteen. Ascent Native Fuel Whey (Chocolate) and Ascent Plant Protein (Vanilla Bean) are both on the tested list. Neither made the top 16.

That isn’t a failing grade. Most of the 165 didn’t make the top 16, including well-known brands. But it does mean the one independent test we know of didn’t single Ascent out as unusually clean, and the figures aren’t public, so there’s nothing to compare against CR’s results.

What Is Native Whey? (And Does It Affect Lead?)

Most whey protein is recovered from the liquid left over after cheesemaking. Native whey is filtered directly from milk, so it skips the cheesemaking step and sees less heat. Ascent markets this as cleaner and less processed.

Claim Fair? Does it tell you about lead?
“Less processed” Yes, fewer steps than cheese whey No
“Less heat, intact protein” Yes No
“No artificial sweeteners or flavors” Yes No
“Grass-fed” / “no rBGH” Describes the herd No

Lead in dairy comes from the cow’s environment: soil, water and feed. How the whey is separated from milk doesn’t add or remove it. What helps Ascent is the category: CR found plant-based products averaged about nine times more lead than dairy-based ones, and the lowest-lead products in its October round were mostly whey-based. Category averages don’t clear any single brand, though. That takes a test result.

Informed Sport: What It Covers

Ascent’s whey is listed in the Informed Sport database, with batch testing on record. That’s a genuine credential:

  • What it does: tests product batches for substances banned in sport. If you’re a drug-tested athlete, it matters.
  • What it doesn’t do: publish a lead, cadmium or arsenic figure per serving. You can’t compare an Informed Sport product against CR’s 0.5 µg/day level of concern.

The same is true of NSF Certified for Sport. Both are banned-substance programs first. Neither replaces a published heavy metal result.

Should You Buy Ascent?

Ascent is a reasonable choice if:

  • You’re a drug-tested athlete and want Informed Sport certification
  • You want a whey with no artificial sweeteners and are comfortable with “unknown” on lead
  • You value native whey’s taste or texture

Pick an alternative if:

  • You want a published lead number before you buy
  • You’re pregnant, buying for a teen, or using protein powder more than once a day, where a known low result matters more

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ascent protein powder safe for lead?

Unknown. No lead figure for Ascent has been published. Consumer Reports hasn’t tested it. Clean Label Project included Ascent Native Fuel Whey (Chocolate) in its 165-product protein study, but it was not among the 16 products CLP listed as lowest in contaminants, and CLP doesn’t publish per-product results on that list. Whey as a category tested lowest in Consumer Reports’ data.

Did Consumer Reports test Ascent protein powder?

No. Ascent wasn’t in Consumer Reports’ October 2025 round (23 products) or its January 2026 round (5 powders).

Did Clean Label Project test Ascent?

Yes. CLP’s protein powder study lists Ascent Native Fuel Whey (Chocolate) and Ascent Plant Protein (Vanilla Bean) among 165 products tested. Neither appears on CLP’s Clean Sixteen, the 16 products it found lowest in contaminants. Not making the top 16 out of 165 doesn’t mean a product failed, and CLP doesn’t publish the individual numbers there.

Is Ascent Informed Sport certified?

Yes. Ascent’s whey is listed in the Informed Sport database, which tests batches for substances banned in sport. That’s valuable for drug-tested athletes, but Informed Sport doesn’t publish a per-serving lead figure, so it isn’t a substitute for a heavy metal result.

What is native whey protein?

Native whey is filtered directly from milk rather than recovered as a byproduct of cheesemaking. It goes through less heat and processing. That affects protein structure and taste, not heavy metals, which come from the cow’s environment and feed. Native processing neither adds nor removes lead.

What are lower-lead alternatives to Ascent with published results?

Whey powders Consumer Reports rated better for daily use include Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard 100% Whey (56% of CR’s level of concern) and BSN Syntha-6 (46%) from the October 2025 round, and Clean Simple Eats (0.21 µg) and Equate Whey (0.27 µg) from the January 2026 round.

The Bottom Line

Ascent is a well-made, fairly priced native whey with Informed Sport certification, and whey is the lowest-lead protein category in CR’s data. What it doesn’t have is a published lead number: CR hasn’t tested it, and CLP tested it without naming it among the cleanest 16. If a known result matters to you, ON Gold Standard, BSN Syntha-6, Clean Simple Eats and Equate all have one.
