Dymatize ISO 100 Review: Was It Ever Actually Tested for Lead? (2026)

📅 Updated: July 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read 📊 Consumer Reports (Oct 2025 + Jan 2026) & Clean Label Project

✅ Direct Answer: Was Dymatize ISO 100 Tested for Lead?

No. Consumer Reports never tested Dymatize ISO 100. They tested the Dymatize Super Mass Gainer — a completely different product.

  • What CR tested: Dymatize Super Mass Gainer (Gourmet Vanilla, 333 g serving) — 25% of CR’s level of concern for lead. Cleared for up to 4 servings/day. Second-cleanest result in the entire study.
  • What CR did NOT test: ISO 100 (hydrolyzed whey isolate, ~110 cal/serving). It appears in neither the October 2025 round of 23 products nor the January 2026 round of 5 powders.
  • What ISO 100 does have: a Clean Label Project Purity Award — genuine independent certification screening for heavy metals. But Clean Label Project issues a pass/fail certification, not a published per-serving lead figure.
  • Our rating for ISO 100: ✅ Cleared on certification — not verified by a published test result. That distinction is the whole point of this page.

📌 Correction (July 2026)

An earlier version of this page, and several others on this site, listed “Dymatize ISO 100” as Consumer Reports #2. That was wrong. The product CR ranked #2 is the Super Mass Gainer. We had transferred a result between two products from the same brand — which is precisely the error this site exists to call out.

We’ve corrected it everywhere. It’s the same mistake as assuming Premier Protein’s powder result applies to their RTD shakes. It doesn’t, and this didn’t either.

Why This Distinction Actually Matters

If you search “Dymatize ISO 100 lead,” you will find plenty of pages telling you it ranked #2 in Consumer Reports testing. It didn’t. It wasn’t in the study.

⚠️ Serving size is why you can’t transfer the result

Lead figures are reported per serving. The Super Mass Gainer’s serving is 333 g. ISO 100’s is about 31 g — roughly a tenth the mass. Even if the two powders had identical lead concentration, their per-serving figures would differ by an order of magnitude. Different formula, different ingredients, different serving. The number simply does not carry over.

So What Does ISO 100’s Certification Actually Tell You?

ISO 100 holds a Clean Label Project Purity Award. That is real, and it is independent. But it’s worth being precise about what it does and doesn’t give you.

💡 A certification is a pass/fail. A test result is a number.

Clean Label Project screens products for 400+ contaminants — heavy metals, pesticides, plasticizers, mycotoxins — and awards certification to products that clear their thresholds. Fewer than 30% of protein products tested earn it. Passing is meaningful.

What it does not do is publish a per-serving lead figure the way Consumer Reports does. So you can say “ISO 100 passed an independent heavy-metals screen.” You cannot say “ISO 100 contains X µg of lead,” because nobody has published that number.

What we can say honestly about ISO 100

  • It passed an independent heavy-metals screen. That's real evidence, and most products don't have it.
  • Its base is inherently low-risk. Lead in protein powder comes overwhelmingly from soil uptake in plants. Whey isolate is dairy-derived and ultra-filtered. Consumer Reports found every one of the top-7 cleanest products was whey-based; the bottom of the table is dominated by plant proteins.
  • The manufacturer has a proven result. Dymatize produced the second-cleanest product in CR's entire study. That says something real about their quality control.
  • But there is no published lead figure for ISO 100 itself. We are not going to invent one, and we are not going to borrow the Super Mass Gainer's.

✅ Our verdict

Cleared — on certification, not on a published test result. If you want a whey protein with an actual published lead number from an independent test, Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard (CR #5, 56% of CR’s level of concern) has one — and costs less. If you want ISO 100 specifically for the hydrolyzed isolate, you're not taking a meaningful risk. Just know what you're relying on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did Consumer Reports test Dymatize ISO 100?

No. ISO 100 appears in neither CR round — not the October 2025 study of 23 products, not the January 2026 study of 5 powders. The Dymatize product CR tested was the Super Mass Gainer. Any page telling you ISO 100 “ranked #2 in Consumer Reports” is transferring a result between products. We used to be one of them. We fixed it.

Does Dymatize ISO 100 have lead?

Nobody has published a figure, so we don’t know — and neither does anyone else. ISO 100 holds a Clean Label Project Purity Award, meaning it passed an independent heavy-metals screen. But Clean Label Project issues a certification, not a per-serving lead number. Passing the screen is real evidence. It is not the same as a published test result.

Is ISO 100 safe?

The evidence is positive, but indirect. It passed an independent heavy-metals screen, its whey isolate base is inherently low-risk (lead comes mostly from plant soil uptake), and its manufacturer produced the second-cleanest product CR tested. What it lacks is a published number. We rate it cleared on certification, and we think you should know that’s what you’re relying on.

ISO 100 vs Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard?

ON has better evidence and a lower price. Why does the serving size matter so much?

Should I switch from Naked Nutrition or Huel?
