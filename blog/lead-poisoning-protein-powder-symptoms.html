Lead Poisoning from Protein Powder: Symptoms, Testing & When to See a Doctor

Published October 17, 2025 | Updated October 21, 2026 | 9 min read | By Ray Rothwell

The short answer

Most protein powders Consumer Reports tested add a small amount of lead to your diet. A few add a lot: CR measured 7.7 µg per serving in Naked Nutrition Vegan Mass Gainer and 6.3 µg in Huel Black Edition. One serving of either comes close to the FDA’s interim reference level of 8.8 µg/day from all food for women of childbearing age.

Product testing can’t tell you whether your own blood lead went up. Most people with low-level lead exposure have no symptoms, so a blood lead test from your doctor is the only way to know. If you’re pregnant, a child has been drinking a high-lead product, or you used one daily for months, that test is worth asking for.

Can Protein Powder Cause Lead Poisoning?

Lead builds up from many small sources: food, water, old paint, soil, some jobs and hobbies. Protein powder is one more source, and for most products CR tested it’s a small one. The concern is daily use of the few products that tested far higher than the rest.

Two benchmarks, and what each one means

  • CR’s level of concern: 0.5 µg/day. This is the threshold Consumer Reports used to grade products, and it’s deliberately conservative. A product at 200% of it is not “twice the poisoning dose.” CR states that no Prop 65 judgment can be made from its findings.
  • FDA interim reference levels: 2.2 µg/day for children, 8.8 µg/day for women of childbearing age. These are the FDA’s ceilings for lead from all food combined. They’re set so that staying under them keeps blood lead well below the level public health agencies flag.

What this means in practice: one daily serving of Naked or Huel uses up most of a woman’s FDA reference level before any other food. Two servings go over it. For a child, a single serving of either is roughly three times the FDA’s 2.2 µg/day reference level. Most other products CR tested sit far below these figures.

Symptoms of Lead Exposure in Adults

The most important fact about symptoms: low-level lead exposure usually doesn’t cause any. Its effects at low levels show up over years, as higher blood pressure or reduced kidney function, not as something you’d notice day to day.

Symptoms the CDC and NIH associate with higher lead levels in adults

  • Raised blood pressure
  • Joint and muscle pain, or weakness in the fingers, wrists or ankles
  • Headaches
  • Trouble with memory, attention or concentration
  • Mood changes
  • Abdominal pain, constipation, loss of appetite
  • Anemia (found on a blood test)
  • Reduced fertility in men and women; miscarriage or preterm birth

Don’t diagnose yourself from this list

Every item above has many common causes, from poor sleep to stress to other medical conditions. Having several of them doesn’t mean you have lead poisoning, and having none doesn’t mean your blood lead is normal. If you’re worried, the useful next step is a conversation with your doctor and, if they agree, a blood test.

Seek care promptly for severe abdominal pain, confusion, seizures, or a child who has swallowed something that may contain lead. US Poison Control is free and available 24/7 at 1-800-222-1222.

Who Should Talk to a Doctor About Testing

You don’t need a test just because you’ve used protein powder. Ask your doctor about a blood lead test if any of these apply:

  • You’re pregnant, breastfeeding or planning a pregnancy and used one of the higher-lead products above. Lead crosses the placenta, and lead stored in bone can be released during pregnancy.
  • A child or teen has been drinking a higher-lead product. Children absorb more of the lead they swallow than adults do, and the CDC has identified no safe blood lead level in children. (See our guide to protein powder for teens and kids.)
  • You used Naked, Huel, Garden of Life Sport or a similar high-lead product daily for months, especially more than one serving a day.
  • You have other lead sources: a home built before 1978 with old paint, lead service pipes, work in construction or battery recycling, shooting ranges, or imported spices, cosmetics or cookware.
  • You have kidney disease, which can affect how your body clears lead.

What to tell your doctor

  1. The exact product name and flavor (a photo of the tub label helps)
  2. How long you used it and how many servings a day
  3. That Consumer Reports tested it, with a link to CR’s October 2025 article
  4. Any other lead sources from the list above
  5. Whether you’re pregnant or planning to be

How the Blood Lead Test Works

It’s a standard blood draw, ideally from a vein. Fingerstick screening tests exist, but a raised fingerstick result is usually confirmed with a venous sample. Many insurers cover the test when a doctor orders it.

  • 3.5 µg/dL is the current US blood lead reference value (CDC/NIOSH). A result above it means your level is higher than about 97.5% of people. It calls for follow-up and a search for the source. It isn’t a diagnosis of poisoning on its own.
  • Your doctor interprets the number in light of your history, other test results and whether you’re pregnant. Follow their plan rather than a chart online, including this one.
  • Timing matters. Lead in blood has a half-life of roughly a month in adults. If you stopped the product months ago, your blood level may have come down even though some lead has moved into bone, where it can stay for years to decades.

What Treatment Actually Looks Like

For most people, the treatment is removing the source. Stop the product, check for other sources, and let your doctor decide whether and when to retest.

  • Chelation is a prescription treatment that binds lead so the body can excrete it. Doctors reserve it for high blood lead levels, generally far above what dietary exposure produces, because it has real side effects. It is a specialist’s decision, not something to seek out on your own.
  • Diet helps with what you absorb next, not what’s already there. Getting enough calcium, iron and vitamin C reduces how much lead your gut absorbs. That’s standard CDC advice for children, and it’s part of CDC guidance in pregnancy.
  • Skip “detox” products. Cleanses, detox kits, extra water, clay, charcoal and over-the-counter chelation supplements don’t remove lead, and some contain lead themselves.

If You’ve Been Using a High-Lead Product

  1. Stop using it. There’s no need to finish the tub. Don’t give it away.
  2. Switch to a product CR found low in lead (see below).
  3. Talk to your doctor if any of the testing criteria above apply to you. Bring the product details.
  4. Don’t panic. Blood lead falls once the source is gone, and one product is rarely someone’s only exposure. Your doctor can help you find any others.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can protein powder give you lead poisoning?

Protein powder adds to your lead exposure, and a few products add a lot. Consumer Reports’ October 2025 testing found Naked Nutrition Vegan Mass Gainer at 7.7 µg of lead per serving and Huel Black Edition at 6.3 µg. One serving of either comes close to the FDA’s interim reference level of 8.8 µg per day from all food for women of childbearing age, and two servings exceed it. Whether that raised anyone’s blood lead isn’t something product testing can show. A blood lead test is the only way to know your own level.

What are the symptoms of lead poisoning in adults?

Most adults with low-level lead exposure have no symptoms. At higher levels, the CDC and NIH list raised blood pressure, joint and muscle pain, headaches, memory and concentration problems, mood changes, abdominal pain, and reduced fertility. These symptoms are common and have many causes, so they can’t confirm or rule out lead exposure. A blood test can.

Should I get a blood lead test?

Ask your doctor about a blood lead test if you are pregnant or planning a pregnancy and used a high-lead product, if a child has been drinking it, if you used one of the highest-lead products daily for months, or if you have other lead sources such as an older home, a job or hobby involving lead, or imported spices or cookware. It is a standard blood draw.

What blood lead level is considered high?

U.S. public health agencies use 3.5 µg/dL as the blood lead reference value. A result above it means your level is higher than about 97.5% of people and is worth a follow-up and a look for the source. It is not by itself a diagnosis of lead poisoning. Your doctor interprets the result.

How long does lead stay in your body?

Lead in blood has a half-life of roughly a month in adults, so blood levels fall over weeks to months once the source is removed. Lead that has moved into bone can stay for years to decades and is released slowly, which is why a normal blood test months after stopping a product doesn’t mean you were never exposed.

Does chelation or a detox help?

For most people, removing the source is the treatment. Chelation is a prescription treatment that doctors reserve for high blood lead levels, generally far above what dietary exposure produces, and it carries real risks. Detox kits, cleanses, extra water and supplements do not remove lead. Eating enough calcium, iron and vitamin C can reduce how much new lead you absorb.

I used Naked Nutrition Vegan Mass Gainer or Huel Black Edition every day. What should I do?

Stop using it and switch to a product Consumer Reports found low in lead. Then tell your doctor how long and how often you used it and ask whether a blood lead test makes sense for you, especially if you are pregnant, planning a pregnancy, or a child was also drinking it.

I’m pregnant and used a high-lead protein powder. What should I do?

Stop using it and tell your OB or midwife at your next visit, or sooner if you have concerns. Lead crosses the placenta, and lead stored in bone can be released during pregnancy, so ask about a blood lead test. Don’t stop eating; adequate calcium in pregnancy is part of CDC guidance on lead.

Which protein powders had the lowest lead in Consumer Reports’ testing?

In CR’s October 2025 round, MuscleTech 100% Mass Gainer was the only product with lead not detected, followed by Dymatize Super Mass Gainer (25% of CR’s level of concern), Momentous Whey Protein Isolate (30%, since discontinued), BSN Syntha-6 (46%) and Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard 100% Whey powder (56%). In CR’s separate January 2026 round, Clean Simple Eats (0.21 µg) and Equate Whey (0.27 µg) were lowest.

The Bottom Line

  • A few protein powders carry a lot of lead, enough that a daily serving uses up most of the FDA’s reference level for women of childbearing age. Most of the products CR tested carry far less.
  • Symptoms aren’t a reliable signal. Low-level exposure usually causes none, and the symptoms of higher exposure have many other causes.
  • A blood lead test is the only way to know, and it’s worth asking your doctor about if you’re pregnant, a child has been drinking a high-lead product, or you used one daily for months.
  • Removing the source is the main treatment. Chelation is reserved for high levels and prescribed by a doctor. Detox products don’t work.
