Is Legion Whey+ Safe? Independent Lead Testing Results

📅 Updated: August 12, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read 📊 Labdoor Independent Testing

✅ Direct Answer: Is Legion Whey+ Safe?

Yes, based on independent testing. Labdoor tested Legion Whey+ and found lead, arsenic, cadmium and mercury all below the lab's limit of quantification - effectively non-detect - in a test dated March 6, 2026. One important catch: despite Legion's marketing, Whey+ is not NSF Certified for Sport. It's made in an NSF-certified facility, which is a different (and weaker) claim.

The NSF Certification Catch

Legion markets Whey+ as being "made in an NSF-certified facility." That's a real claim, but it's easy to misread as a product safety certification. It isn't.

⚠️ Facility Certification vs. Product Certification

NSF-certified facility: The manufacturing plant follows Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards. This says something about the facility's process controls - not about what's actually in any specific tub.

NSF Certified for Sport: The finished product itself is tested lot-by-lot for heavy metals, label accuracy, and 290+ substances banned in competitive sport. This is what Thorne Whey Isolate carries, for comparison.

Legion Whey+ does not currently appear in NSF's Certified for Sport product database. The Labdoor result above is real and independent, but it's a single point-in-time test, not the every-lot testing that NSF Sport certification guarantees.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Legion Whey+ safe from heavy metals?

Yes, based on independent testing. Labdoor found lead, arsenic, cadmium and mercury all below the lab's limit of quantification in a test dated March 6, 2026.

Has Consumer Reports tested Legion Whey+?

No. It wasn't included in either CR testing round. The Labdoor result is a separate, still-credible independent source.

Is Legion Whey+ NSF Certified for Sport?

No. It's made in an NSF-certified facility (a GMP audit), which is different from NSF Certified for Sport (a product-level, every-lot contaminant and banned-substance test). Whey+ does not currently appear in NSF's Sport database.

Is Legion Whey+ worth the price?

At ~$1.99/serving, it costs more than Body Fortress or ON Gold Standard, both independently verified and cheaper. Legion's case rests on grass-fed sourcing and its Labdoor result, not on cheaper or more thorough safety testing.
