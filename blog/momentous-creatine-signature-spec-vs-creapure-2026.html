Momentous Dropped Creapure®. Here's What "Signature Spec" Actually Is.

📅 Published: July 12, 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read 📊 Sourcing change analysis · NSF & independent testing

⚡ Direct Answer: Is Momentous Still Creapure®?

No. Momentous stopped using Creapure® in June 2026. It now uses an in-house standard called Signature Spec™ — creatine made in a pharmaceutical facility to a specification Momentous owns. It remains NSF Certified for Sport, and Momentous has actually added testing (PFAS, microplastics, blind off-the-shelf purchase) that neither Creapure® nor NSF requires. This is not a safety downgrade. But if you bought Momentous because it was Creapure®, that reason no longer exists — and almost nobody has told you. Is it actually better? →

Momentous said this out loud, on its own product pages: "Momentous no longer sources our Creatine from Creapure®." Yet half the internet — retailer listings, "best creatine" lists, review sites, and until this week our own Creapure® database — still tells you it does.

What Actually Changed

In June 2026, Momentous announced a complete reformulation of its best-selling product. The headline nobody covered properly: Creapure® is gone.

⚠️ The claims Momentous is making

  • 10× fewer impurities than its previous creatine — and its previous creatine was Creapure®. Read that again: they're implicitly claiming to beat the standard they abandoned.
  • 2–5× tighter limits for lead, mercury and cadmium than "the industry's premium standard."
  • Non-detectable arsenic, cadmium, lead and mercury; 99.8% potency accuracy.

Is Signature Spec Actually Better Than Creapure®?

This is the question that matters, and the honest answer has two halves that point in opposite directions.

✅ Where it's genuinely stronger

  • Six-stage testing across four independent labs — Eurofins, Light Labs, NSF, and SuppCo
  • PFAS + microplastics screening — neither Creapure® nor NSF requires this. Almost nobody in the category does it.
  • SuppCo's TESTED program buys the product anonymously off the shelf, like a customer would. That closes the loophole where a brand submits a pristine sample and ships something else.
  • Still NSF Certified for Sport — every batch, banned substances, heavy metals, label accuracy
  • Testing runs from raw material through to packaged goods, catching contamination in transit

⚠️ Where the skepticism is fair

  • "A spec we own" — Creapure® is defined by an external body (AlzChem). Signature Spec is defined by Momentous. They set the bar they're clearing.
  • "10× fewer impurities" is a self-comparison against their own prior product, using their own spec. No independent body has adjudicated that claim.
  • Creapure® is a 30-year-old known quantity with a public DCD/DHT testing standard. Signature Spec is weeks old.
  • Dropping a licensed premium ingredient usually lowers input cost — and the retail price didn't drop.

💡 Our read: the specification is self-defined, but the verification is not

That distinction is everything, and it's where most takes on this will go wrong.

Yes — Momentous wrote its own standard, and you should be skeptical of any brand that grades its own homework. But they didn't stop there. They kept NSF Certified for Sport (external, every batch), added three more independent labs, and — crucially — signed up for blind off-the-shelf purchase testing, which is the single hardest form of verification to game. A brand cheating its own spec would not invite an anonymous buyer to test the retail product. On the evidence available, this looks like a real upgrade rather than a cost-cutting exercise dressed as one.

It's Momentous's own creatine specification — a standard the company owns, produced in a pharmaceutical facility and purified with reverse-osmosis water filtration instead of chemical washing. Momentous claims 10× fewer impurities than its previous (Creapure®) creatine and 2–5× tighter heavy-metal limits, plus PFAS and microplastics testing that no standard certification requires.
