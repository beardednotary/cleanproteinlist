It's the best possible result on a heavy-metal panel. It means the lab's instruments — capable of measuring at microgram level — found no measurable lead at all. Not "low." Not "within limits." None found.

Informed Sport: The Certification That Actually Keeps Checking

NOW Sports Whey Protein Isolate carries Informed Sport certification. This is a genuinely strong program, and it's worth understanding why:

✅ What Informed Sport Actually Does

  • Tests every production batch — not a one-time sample
  • Screens for 285+ WADA-banned substances
  • Accredited to ISO 17025, the global lab standard
  • Blind-samples products at retail up to 4× a year — testing what's actually on shelves
  • Results are publicly searchable at informed-sport.com

⚠️ What It Doesn't Do

  • It's a banned-substance program first — heavy metals aren't its headline focus
  • It doesn't publish a per-serving lead figure the way Consumer Reports does
  • Certification covers specific products, not the whole NOW catalog

💡 The Blind Retail Sampling Is the Part That Matters

Most certifications test what a brand sends them. Informed Sport goes and buys the product off a shelf, unannounced, up to four times a year. That closes the loophole where a brand submits a pristine sample and ships something else. Combined with NOW's own ISO-accredited in-house labs — which the company says run 31,000+ tests per month — this is a serious quality-control operation, not a logo.

NOW also holds SQF (Safe Quality Food) certification and manufactures in GMP facilities. And in an unusual regulatory move, NOW classifies its protein powders as conventional foods rather than dietary supplements — a stricter FDA category.

Consumer Reports Didn't Test It. Here's Why That's Fine.

NOW Sports wasn't in Consumer Reports' October 2025 or January 2026 rounds. On most brands, we'd treat that as a genuine gap and say so plainly — that's what we did with Nutricost and Sunwarrior.

NOW is different, because the independent data already exists. Consumer Reports' value is that it buys products anonymously and publishes hard numbers. Labdoor does the same thing — buys off the shelf, tests, publishes the full COA. And Informed Sport's blind retail sampling adds ongoing surveillance that even Consumer Reports doesn't provide.

⚖️ Being Straight About the Limits

A Consumer Reports result would still add something: CR benchmarks lead against California's Prop 65 limit of 0.5 µg/day, which is far stricter than the USP daily limits Labdoor used (5 µg for lead). "Undetected" clears both — but a CR test would let us place NOW precisely alongside Dymatize, ON, and MuscleTech in the rankings. We'd like to see it. We just don't need it to call this one safe.

It's Whey Isolate — the Safest Corner of a Risky Category

Consumer Reports' central finding: whey averaged roughly 9× less lead than plant-based proteins, and 6 of the 7 safest products tested were whey. Cows filter heavy metals out before they reach milk. Plants pull them up from soil.

NOW Sports uses microfiltered whey isolate — the more heavily filtered form — at 25g protein per 28g scoop (an unusually high ~89% protein by weight).

Product-line caveat: Everything on this page applies to NOW Sports Whey Protein Isolate specifically — the product with the Labdoor COA and Informed Sport certification. NOW Foods sells a large catalog including plant-based, pea, rice, and organic proteins, plus a whey concentrate. Informed Sport certification covers specific products, not the entire brand, and we never transfer a result from one product line to another.
