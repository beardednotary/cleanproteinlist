What the Clean Sixteen Is (and Isn’t)

Clean Label Project is a nonprofit that buys products and has them lab-tested for heavy metals and other contaminants. In its protein powder study, CLP tested 165 products and published the full list of what it tested, marking the 16 with the lowest contaminant levels as its Clean Sixteen.

  • It is a meaningful comparative result. Making the top 16 of 165 puts a product well ahead of most of the market.
  • It isn’t a certification. It’s separate from CLP’s certification and Purity Award programs, and it reflects the samples CLP bought for that study, not ongoing batch testing.
  • It isn’t a number. CLP doesn’t publish per-product lead figures on the list, so you can’t place RYSE on Consumer Reports’ 0.5 µg/day scale.
  • It’s flavor-specific. CLP listed products by flavor. Cocoa in particular can carry heavy metals, which may be why chocolate flavors often test differently from vanilla or fruit flavors of the same brand.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is RYSE protein powder safe for lead?

One RYSE flavor has a strong independent result. Clean Label Project tested 165 protein powders and named RYSE Loaded Protein in Jet-Puffed Marshmallow flavor among the 16 with the lowest contaminant levels (its Clean Sixteen). CLP doesn’t publish the individual figures, and Consumer Reports hasn’t tested RYSE, so there’s no per-serving lead number to compare.

Are all RYSE flavors on the Clean Sixteen?

No. CLP tested two RYSE Loaded Protein flavors. Jet-Puffed Marshmallow is on the Clean Sixteen. Chocolate Cookie Blast was tested but isn’t on the list. Other flavors weren’t in CLP’s study. Flavorings, especially cocoa, can change heavy metal content, so a result for one flavor doesn’t transfer to another.

Is the Clean Sixteen a certification?

No. The Clean Sixteen is a ranking from a single CLP study: the 16 products with the lowest contaminant levels out of 165 tested. It’s separate from CLP’s certification and Purity Award programs. It’s a strong comparative result, but not a promise about every batch.

Did Consumer Reports test RYSE?

No. RYSE wasn’t in Consumer Reports’ October 2025 round (23 products) or its January 2026 round (5 powders).

Which proteins have both a Clean Sixteen listing and a Consumer Reports result?

Two brands have both, though for different flavors. Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard 100% Whey is on the Clean Sixteen in Vanilla Ice Cream, and CR tested the chocolate powder at 56% of its level of concern. Premier Protein 100% Whey powder is on the Clean Sixteen in Vanilla Milkshake, and CR tested the chocolate powder at 0.38 µg (77%).

Is RYSE worth the price compared with other low-lead proteins?

RYSE runs about $1.50 to $1.80 a serving. Body Fortress Super Advanced Whey (Vanilla) is also on the Clean Sixteen at about $0.67 a serving, and Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard has a Clean Sixteen listing plus a Consumer Reports result at about $0.75. If you like RYSE’s flavors, the Marshmallow flavor has the independent result to back it up.

The Bottom Line

RYSE Loaded Protein in Jet-Puffed Marshmallow made Clean Label Project’s list of the 16 cleanest proteins out of 165, which is a better independent result than most brands can point to. It’s a ranking rather than a published number, it covers one flavor, and Consumer Reports hasn’t tested RYSE. If you want the same kind of evidence for less, Body Fortress has it; if you want a CR number too, ON Gold Standard and Premier Protein powder have both.
