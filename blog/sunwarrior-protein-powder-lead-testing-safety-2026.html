Is Sunwarrior Protein Safe? Lead Testing & Heavy Metal Analysis

📅 Published: July 10, 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read 📊 Plant protein category analysis · No CR or CLP data available

❓ Direct Answer: Is Sunwarrior Safe?

Unverified — and that's a choice Sunwarrior made. Sunwarrior admits its plant proteins contain heavy metals (credit for honesty) and says an ISO-certified lab tests every batch. But it does not publish its lead numbers, did not earn a Clean Label Project Purity Award when tested, and hasn't been tested by Consumer Reports. Its Warrior Blend (pea/hemp) is meaningfully lower-risk than its Classic line (rice). We rate it Untested — plant category risk, no published numbers. See verified plant alternatives →

Sunwarrior does something most brands won't: it tells you straight out that there are heavy metals in its protein. Then it does something most brands also won't — it declines to tell you how much.

⚠️ The Purity Award Absence Is Not Neutral

There's a difference between a brand Clean Label Project never tested and a brand it tested that didn't earn the award. Sunwarrior is the second kind. A brand that's simply never been screened has an information gap. A brand that was screened and didn't get the seal has a result — just not one anybody published. That's a meaningful distinction, and it's why we won't call Sunwarrior "simply untested."

The "Less Lead Than an Avocado" Argument — and Why It Doesn't Work

Sunwarrior's standing public defense is a comparison to whole foods. Their line, repeated across their site and press responses: their protein contains 68% less lead than an average avocado, 76% less cadmium than a cup of spinach, and 47% less arsenic than a peach.

It sounds reassuring. It's also the wrong comparison, for three reasons:

Why the food comparison fails

  1. Nobody eats an avocado twice a day, every day, for years. Protein powder is a daily habit product. The entire concern with lead is cumulative chronic exposure, not a single serving. Comparing one scoop to one avocado ignores the variable that matters: frequency.
  2. It compares against the wrong benchmark. The question isn't "is this less than a vegetable?" It's "how does this compare to other protein powders — and to the 0.5 µg/day Prop 65 threshold?" Whey proteins routinely come back below detection. That's the real bar, and an avocado isn't it.
  3. It's an argument you make when you won't show the number. If the lead figure were genuinely low, publishing it would be far more persuasive than any produce analogy. Transparent Labs publishes lot-matched COAs. Sunwarrior could too.

To be fair to Sunwarrior: the underlying claim that all plant proteins carry some heavy metals is true, and their willingness to say so is more honest than the brands that market themselves as "clean" while facing lead lawsuits. But honesty about the existence of metals isn't the same as transparency about the amount.

The Organic Trap: Certification That Says Nothing About Lead

Sunwarrior leads with USDA Organic and Non-GMO Project Verified. Both are real certifications. Neither tests for heavy metals.

❌ What "Organic" Actually Covers

USDA Organic governs pesticides and farming practices. It has nothing to do with lead, cadmium, arsenic, or mercury. Plants pull metals out of soil regardless of whether that soil is certified organic.

Worse: Clean Label Project found organic protein powders averaged roughly 3× more lead than conventional ones. The organic seal is, if anything, a mild negative signal on heavy metals — and it's the exact seal at the center of the Orgain and Garden of Life lawsuits.

This is the single most misunderstood thing in the category, and it's why plant/organic proteins keep drawing litigation while whey does not.

Not All Sunwarrior Is Equal: Warrior Blend vs. Classic

This is the most actionable thing on this page, and almost nobody makes the distinction. Sunwarrior's product lines carry different levels of risk — because they're built on different plants.

💡 Why Rice Protein Is the Problem Child

Rice pulls arsenic out of soil and water more aggressively than nearly any other crop. It's grown in flooded paddies, and that standing water mobilizes arsenic into the plant. Concentrate rice into a protein isolate and you concentrate the arsenic with it.

That's why Warrior Blend's pea-and-hemp base is a genuinely better starting point than the rice-based Classic line. It sidesteps the single biggest metals problem in plant protein. (The goji berry in the blend is a small complicating factor — goji can carry its own metals — but the pea base is still the cleaner foundation.)

We do not transfer results or assumptions between these lines, and neither should you. If you use Sunwarrior and won't switch brands, Warrior Blend over Classic is the single best change you can make.

The 2023 Prop 65 Settlement — What It Was, and What It Wasn't

Setting the record straight, because this gets misreported. Sunwarrior entered a 2023 California Proposition 65 settlement over lead. That settlement concerned its Beauty Greens Collagen Booster — not its protein powders. The company denied the allegations and paid a small civil penalty.

It is worth knowing that the company has been in Prop 65 crosshairs before. It is not evidence of a lead problem in Warrior Blend or Classic, and we won't present it as such. Different product, different testing, different question. Applying it to the protein line would be exactly the kind of cross-product-line transfer we criticize other sites for.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Sunwarrior protein powder safe?

Unverified. Sunwarrior acknowledges heavy metals are present and says an ISO-certified lab tests every batch against EU, WHO and FDA limits. But it doesn't publish the numbers, didn't earn a Clean Label Project Purity Award when tested, and hasn't been tested by Consumer Reports. We rate it Untested — plant category risk, no published numbers.

Does Sunwarrior protein have lead?

Yes — and Sunwarrior says so openly. Every plant protein does; plants absorb metals from soil. The unresolved question is how much. Sunwarrior won't publish that figure, and no independent organization has. "There is lead, trust us that it's fine" is a weaker position than "here is the number."

Is Warrior Blend safer than Sunwarrior Classic?

On arsenic, almost certainly yes. Warrior Blend is pea + hemp; Classic is brown rice. Rice absorbs arsenic far more readily than other crops because it's grown in flooded paddies. Concentrating rice into protein concentrates the arsenic too. If you're staying with Sunwarrior, Warrior Blend over Classic is the clearest improvement available.

Doesn't organic mean fewer heavy metals?

No — and this is the most costly misconception in the category. USDA Organic regulates pesticides and farming practices, not heavy metals. Clean Label Project found organic protein powders averaged ~3× more lead than conventional. The organic seal is precisely what's at issue in the Orgain and Garden of Life lawsuits.

Did Sunwarrior lose a lead lawsuit?

It settled a 2023 California Prop 65 case over lead — but for its Beauty Greens Collagen Booster, not its protein. Sunwarrior denied the allegations and paid a small civil penalty. That's real context about the company, but it is not a finding against Warrior Blend or Classic, and we won't imply otherwise.

Is Sunwarrior Informed Choice certified?

Select products are — but read what that covers. Informed Choice tests for banned athletic substances, not heavy metals against a chronic-exposure limit. It's a real certification and a genuine positive for athletes. It is not evidence that lead levels are low, and it shouldn't be read that way.

The Bottom Line on Sunwarrior

Sunwarrior is more honest than most plant brands and less transparent than it should be. It admits heavy metals exist — genuinely more candid than competitors who call themselves "clean" while defending lead lawsuits. But it answers the question "how much?" with an avocado comparison instead of a number, it didn't earn the Clean Label Purity Award when tested, and its COAs sit behind a support request. Meanwhile Truvani passed Consumer Reports and Transparent Labs publishes lot-matched COAs anyone can read. The bar has moved. If you stay, take Warrior Blend over Classic and request your COA.
