Which Protein Brands Are Consumers Actually Worried About?

A recurring index of which protein supplement brands site visitors are checking for heavy-metal contamination safety — built from real visitor submissions, not brand popularity or ad spend.

This edition is built from 3,677 poll submissions collected since November 6, 2025 (3,150 deduplicated, brand-identifiable respondents since the brand question was added in January 2026). Orgain, Premier Protein, Levels, and Naked Nutrition hold the top four for a third straight edition. The biggest mover this period: Transparent Labs, up from #13 to #9.

Every brand-safety article on this site carries a small embedded poll: "What protein do you currently use?" It was built to help individual readers get a fast answer about their own brand. It has quietly become something else — a running record of which brands a large, self-selected population of safety-motivated consumers is worried enough about to go check.

This page pulls that data out of a one-time report and gives it a permanent home. It will be updated on a fixed monthly schedule, not left to go stale. What you're looking at is not a popularity ranking. Brands with the most search volume in the mainstream sense — Optimum Nutrition, Muscle Milk — are not necessarily at the top here. This is a safety-concern index: which brands are people specifically checking on a site built around heavy-metal testing data.

The rule that governs this page: this index ranks brands strictly by how often visitors named them in the poll. No brand can pay to enter it, leave it, or move within it, and none has. If that ever stops being true, this page stops being worth citing — and worth reading.

What Moved

The Story Behind This Edition's Rankings

A quiet period, and a small one. Only 249 new deduplicated respondents arrived between August 30 and October 13, against 2,901 in the August data. That's a fall in site traffic, not a change in method, and it means this edition can only move ranks a little. Read single-place moves as noise. The site was also unreachable for about three days in mid-September (zero submissions September 14–16).

The top eight did not change. Orgain, Premier Protein, Levels, Naked Nutrition, Optimum Nutrition, Garden of Life, Isopure and Nutricost hold the same positions as in the August data. Orgain remains far ahead, but its pull has faded since the summer. It took 24% of all respondents in July, the month the Costco (July 7) and Amazon (July 31) class actions were filed and widely covered, and about 10% in September, back near its spring level. Our one-year retrospective on the Consumer Reports report looks at that pattern in more detail.

Transparent Labs is this edition's clearest mover, up four places from #13 to #9 on 10 new submissions, the fifth-most of any brand this period. We can't point to a news event that explains it, so we're reporting it as measured, without an attributed cause. Ascent also gained two places (#15 to #13) on 9 new submissions.

OWYN, Sunwarrior and Fairlife each slipped two places on two or fewer new submissions apiece. That's other brands growing around them rather than a drop in interest.

Method

How This Index Is Built

The underlying instrument is a short, voluntary poll embedded across CleanProteinList.com's blog content: visitors are asked what protein brand they currently use and whether it's a powder, ready-to-drink shake, or that they're switching brands. The free-text brand field was added to the poll on January 21, 2026; submissions before that date recorded only the product-type answer and are excluded from the brand rankings above.

Limitations: Respondents are self-selected visitors to CleanProteinList.com, arriving primarily via organic search on heavy-metal testing and brand safety content. This population is meaningfully more safety-motivated than the general protein supplement consumer, so this index measures search/concern behavior among that segment — it is not a claim about which brands are least safe, most contaminated, or most popular overall. A brand's presence or absence, and its position, reflects how often visitors named it, nothing else. Normalization is moderate: obvious spelling and capitalization variants and recognizable product-line names are grouped under a parent brand (for example, "ON Gold Standard" under Optimum Nutrition); ambiguous or clearly distinct entries are left ungrouped. Rank-change comparisons are directional; the July 14 baseline used a slightly different top-N cutoff (15 vs. this edition's 20) and a marginally different dedup pass, so treat single-position moves near the bottom of the table as noise and multi-position moves as signal. Data in this edition is current as of October 13, 2026. Method note (October 2026): the normalization pass is now a saved, versioned script rather than a one-off cleanup, so future editions are exactly reproducible. Re-running it on the August data gives 2,901 unique respondents versus the 2,881 originally published, with the same top four in the same order. Mid-table brands shift by a few places, mainly because the script groups more variants (for example, "ON" under Optimum Nutrition and product-line entries under Ancient Nutrition). Moves in this edition are measured against that re-run, not the August page as published.
