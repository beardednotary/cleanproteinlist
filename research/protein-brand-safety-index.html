Which Protein Brands Are Consumers Actually Worried About?

A recurring index of which protein supplement brands site visitors are checking for heavy-metal contamination safety — built from real visitor submissions, not brand popularity or ad spend.

This edition is built from 3,407 poll submissions collected since November 6, 2025 (2,881 deduplicated, brand-identifiable respondents since the brand question was added in January 2026). Orgain, Premier Protein, and Levels remain the three most-searched brands. The biggest mover this period: Garden of Life, up from #7 to #5.

Every brand-safety article on this site carries a small embedded poll: "What protein do you currently use?" It was built to help individual readers get a fast answer about their own brand. It has quietly become something else — a running record of which brands a large, self-selected population of safety-motivated consumers is worried enough about to go check.

This page pulls that data out of a one-time report and gives it a permanent home. It will be updated on a fixed monthly schedule, not left to go stale. What you're looking at is not a popularity ranking. Brands with the most search volume in the mainstream sense — Optimum Nutrition, Muscle Milk — are not necessarily at the top here. This is a safety-concern index: which brands are people specifically checking on a site built around heavy-metal testing data.

The rule that governs this page: this index ranks brands strictly by how often visitors named them in the poll. No brand can pay to enter it, leave it, or move within it, and none has. If that ever stops being true, this page stops being worth citing — and worth reading.

What Moved

The Story Behind This Edition's Rankings

The top four did not change. Orgain, Premier Protein, Levels, and Naked Nutrition held their exact same positions from the July 14 baseline through this edition, with Orgain adding 31 net new submissions and extending its lead over #2 to a wider margin than it held in July. Orgain's continued dominance is worth reading alongside two active pieces of litigation this site already tracks: an Amazon class action and a Costco class action, both filed July 31, 2026, alleging undisclosed lead, cadmium, and arsenic in Orgain Organic Protein Powder. Consumers checking a brand's safety data after seeing it in the news is exactly the behavior this index is built to catch.

Garden of Life is this edition's clearest mover, climbing from #7 to #5 with 12 additional net submissions — including 9 of its 58 total submissions arriving in just the 14 days before this update, a faster recent pace than all but Orgain itself. This tracks with an active, ongoing class action against the brand: DeHerrera v. Garden of Life (C.D. Cal., No. 5:25-cv-03118), filed December 16, 2025 over undisclosed lead in Organic Plant-Based Protein, remained pending as of this site's own lawsuit tracker in August 2026 — see our full breakdown. The case isn't new this period, but continued visibility of an active suit against a major brand is a plausible, honest explanation for sustained search interest — unlike Fairlife, which also gained meaningfully (four spots, #15 to #11) with no comparable event we can point to. We're reporting Fairlife's move as real and measured, without an attributed cause.

Vega posted the largest decline among ranked brands, falling four spots from #13 to #17 on essentially flat submission volume (36 in July, 35 now) — a relative decline driven by other brands growing around it rather than Vega losing ground outright.

Five brands are new to the tracked top 20 this edition: Ascent, Muscle Milk, NOW Sports, MyProtein, and LeanFit. None of these are new to the market or necessarily new to the data — they simply did not place in the July 14 top 15 as previously published. Expanding this edition's table to 20 brands (versus 15 previously) surfaces more of the real field.

Method

How This Index Is Built

The underlying instrument is a short, voluntary poll embedded across CleanProteinList.com's blog content: visitors are asked what protein brand they currently use and whether it's a powder, ready-to-drink shake, or that they're switching brands. The free-text brand field was added to the poll on January 21, 2026; submissions before that date recorded only the product-type answer and are excluded from the brand rankings above.

Limitations: Respondents are self-selected visitors to CleanProteinList.com, arriving primarily via organic search on heavy-metal testing and brand safety content. This population is meaningfully more safety-motivated than the general protein supplement consumer, so this index measures search/concern behavior among that segment — it is not a claim about which brands are least safe, most contaminated, or most popular overall. A brand's presence or absence, and its position, reflects how often visitors named it, nothing else. Normalization is moderate: obvious spelling and capitalization variants and recognizable product-line names are grouped under a parent brand (for example, "ON Gold Standard" under Optimum Nutrition); ambiguous or clearly distinct entries are left ungrouped. Rank-change comparisons are directional; the July 14 baseline used a slightly different top-N cutoff (15 vs. this edition's 20) and a marginally different dedup pass, so treat single-position moves near the bottom of the table as noise and multi-position moves as signal.
