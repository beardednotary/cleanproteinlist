The protein supplement industry is worth more than $25 billion annually, and by most measures it is growing. But something has shifted in the consumer mindset — and it isn't brand preference, flavor innovation, or price sensitivity. It's fear.

Since Consumer Reports and independent testing labs began publishing heavy metal contamination data on popular protein products, a question has been forming among the tens of millions of Americans who consume these products regularly: Is what I'm drinking every morning actually safe?

Most coverage of protein supplement contamination has focused on the products themselves — lab results, lead levels, brand comparisons. Far less attention has been paid to the consumer side of this story: what do people actually know, what are they worried about, and how is it changing the way they shop?

To answer those questions, CleanProteinList.com deployed two independent consumer research instruments beginning in November 2025. The first was a brand interest poll embedded across the site, capturing which brands visitors were actively researching for safety information. The second was a voluntary nine-question behavioral quiz probing usage habits, contamination awareness, budget tolerance, and primary purchase motivations. This report reflects data collected through July 14, 2026 — 2,937 total poll submissions (~2,669 unique respondents) and 286 quiz completions. The April update reflected 1,762 poll submissions and 215 quiz completions; the original February 19 release included approximately 474 poll submissions and 96 quiz completions.

Why this research was updated in July 2026: CleanProteinList.com refreshed this study following renewed public attention to heavy metals in protein supplements, including a proposed class-action lawsuit filed against Costco concerning Orgain Organic Protein Powder. The complaint alleges that Costco sold certain Orgain products without adequately disclosing lead, cadmium, and arsenic; those allegations have not been proven in court. The lawsuit cites testing by Consumer Reports and other independent testing. This survey is independent of the litigation and measures the views and research behavior of CleanProteinList.com visitors.

The July data confirms the original conclusion while adding a clearer shift in behavior: safety remains the defining concern, awareness continues to rise, and more respondents are moving from general concern toward active comparison, brand switching, and searches for the cleanest option.

Section 1

Who Responded: A Profile of Today's Protein Consumer

Before examining what respondents think, it's worth understanding who they are. Both instruments drew from the same population: visitors to CleanProteinList.com who arrived via organic search, primarily on content covering heavy metal contamination, lead testing results, and brand safety comparisons. This self-selected audience skews toward safety-motivated consumers — an important context for interpreting findings.

Quiz Data from the protein safety quiz (n=286) still reflects a habitual consumer base, although the July sample is broader than it was in April. 77.6% use protein supplements at least "often," including 62.9% who consume them daily. Among the 71 responses added after the April cutoff, daily use was lower and research-stage participation was higher, suggesting that the article is reaching more consumers who are still evaluating their options.

The headline safety-concern response declined from 80.0% in April to 76.2% in July, but the action-oriented categories grew. "Find the cleanest option" increased from 10.2% to 12.6%, and "ready to switch brands" rose from 7.9% to 8.7%. Combined, 97.6% of quiz respondents remain safety-motivated. This pattern is consistent with what the awareness data shows in Section 3: as more consumers become informed about contamination risks, they move from broad concern toward more specific action intent. The concern did not fade. It sharpened.

Only 2.4% of respondents cited experienced symptoms as their primary motivation. The safety-driven consumer in this data set remains predominantly proactive, not reactive. Most are researching before a health event forces the issue.

Section 3

Awareness Keeps Rising — but Nearly a Third Remain Uninformed

The awareness trend continued through July. Compared with April, the fully aware group grew again, while both the partially aware and unaware shares declined. The 71 responses collected after April were especially awareness-heavy: nearly half selected the fully aware response.

Quiz Fully aware respondents increased from 27.0% in April to 32.5% in July. The "not aware" group declined from 31.6% to 30.1%, while the partially aware group fell from 32.6% to 29.0%. For context, only 16.7% were fully aware in the original February release.

Section 5

The Awareness Delta: How Knowledge Shapes Purchase Behavior

One of the most commercially significant findings from this research comes from a cross-tabulation of two quiz variables: contamination awareness level and willingness to pay a per-serving premium for verified clean protein. The analysis reveals a behavioral pattern that has direct implications for content strategy, brand communication, and the information gap identified in Section 3.

With awareness subgroups now ranging from n=83 to n=93 for the three primary groups, the cross-tabulation is more stable than in either earlier release. Partially aware consumers remain the most likely to engage with the price question at 62.7%, compared with 46.2% of fully aware respondents and 45.3% of unaware respondents. The mid-discovery consumer is still the most commercially active segment.

The same nuance remains visible in July: partially aware consumers answer the price question most often, but only 46.2% of those who answer accept at least $1.50 per serving. Fully aware respondents engage less often, yet 67.4% of those who do answer accept that price level. This supports a distinction between active comparison shopping and premium commitment.

Key Finding · The Awareness Delta — Confirmed at Scale

The partially aware consumer engages with the price question at a rate about 38% higher than unaware consumers and 36% higher than fully aware consumers. Yet this group remains more price-sensitive than fully aware respondents. They are actively evaluating options, which makes clear testing evidence and direct comparisons especially important.

Section 7

What This Means: Four Takeaways from the Data

1. Safety-motivated consumers are not a niche.

When 97.6% of respondents fall somewhere inside the safety-motivated cluster, safety is not a secondary feature for this audience. It is the central reason they are researching products.

2. The information gap is narrowing, not closed.

Awareness improved meaningfully between February 19, 2026 and July 14, 2026, but 31.6% of respondents still report no awareness of documented contamination concerns. The market is learning, but it is not yet informed.

3. Brand trust is still up for grabs.

Orgain and Premier Protein clearly lead safety searches, but the field behind them remains fragmented. Levels and Naked Nutrition have grown, and dozens of additional brands continue to appear in the broader data set.

4. Media coverage keeps creating measurable behavior.

The updated poll data continues to reflect what the first release showed: when contamination coverage or social attention lands on a brand, consumers go looking for answers. Search behavior is tracking the downstream effect of safety journalism in real time.
